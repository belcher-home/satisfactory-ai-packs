# MKI Tools

Tools for shaping the world, by MidKnight Industries. `MKITools.uplugin` declares `SemVersion` (currently `0.3.0`). It requires only SML `^3.12.0`; it does not need MKI Roads or MKI Bridge, and neither of them needs it.

## Terrain dismantle mode

The dismantle gun gets a third mode, **Terrain**, after the vanilla Default and Blueprint modes. Switch to it the way you switch to Blueprint dismantle. In Terrain mode the gun aims only at level rocks: the rock under the crosshair is outlined, and holding fire on it for half a second removes it (the dismantle progress bar shows the hold). In the Default and Blueprint modes nothing changes.

- **What it removes:** plain rocks, arches, cliffs and boulders placed in the map (the engine's own `StaticMeshActor`, static, placed in a level). It never touches buildables, nobelisk-destructible rocks, resource deposits, foliage (the chainsaw removes it), resource nodes and their meshes, the landscape, or anything spawned while playing. Reach is 150 m.
- **How:** a removed rock is hidden and loses its collision. It is never deleted, so it can always be restored exactly. There is no refund.
- **Saves and streaming:** the removed rocks are saved with the game. The world streams rocks out and back in as you move, and every time an area loads, its removed rocks are hidden again.
- **Multiplayer:** the host keeps the list and every client applies it. A client's click is checked again on the host (the same rules, and the player's reach).
- **From far away:** the world partition draws distant areas as merged HLOD meshes. Where the HLOD keeps a removed rock as an instance of the same mesh, that instance is hidden too, again each time the HLOD streams in. Where the HLOD drew the rock as a simplified mesh of its own (one that fits inside the rock), that mesh is hidden too. Where a rock is merged into a larger far-view mesh with the terrain around it, it may still show from far away; it is gone (and has no collision) once its area loads around you.
- **Restoring:** MKI Bridge's `POST /v1/terrain/restore-rock` (and the MCP `terrain_rocks` tool, `action: "restore"`) brings a rock back by its actor name; `GET /v1/terrain/removed-rocks` lists them.

## How it works

- `UMKITerrainDismantleMode` is a native dismantle mode descriptor; the module appends it to `UFGBuildGunStateDismantle::GetSupportedBuildModes`.
- In Terrain mode, hooks on the dismantle state's `TickState` and `PrimaryFire` run `FMKITerrainDismantle` instead of vanilla targeting: a trace from the player's view, `FMKITerrainRules`, the dismantle outline, and on click `UMKIToolsRemoteCallObject::Server_RemoveRock`. `Config/AccessTransformers.ini` makes `FMKITerrainDismantle` a friend of the vanilla state, so it can clear the vanilla aim when Terrain mode takes over.
- `AMKITerrainSubsystem` (an SML mod subsystem, spawned on the server and replicated) owns the saved list and applies it to every level the world adds. It also answers MKI Bridge, which calls its `Bridge*` functions by reflection.

## Auto pickup

Hold **E** for a second while looking at nothing E can use (the sky, the ground, a wall): a progress bar fills under the crosshair, and the game's own auto pickup turns on, with its "Auto-Pickup Active" indicator above the hotbar. While it is on, whatever pickable you look at (leaves, wood, mycelia, berries, nuts, mushrooms, flower petals, item pickups) is picked up every half second, through the game's own pickup (its rules, inventory checks and animation), and nothing happens while you look at nothing pickable. Hold E again to turn it off (while it is on, only a machine, container, vehicle or door under the crosshair keeps E the game's own). Looking at anything E can use while it is off (a machine, a bush, a pickup), E is the game's own, tapped or held.

The game has this auto pickup in its foliage pickup proxy (`AFGFoliagePickup`), but on keyboard and mouse nothing turns it on: its toggle action is bound only on a gamepad, and mapping E to it did nothing. `UMKIAutoPickupSubsystem` times the hold, draws its bar as a small Slate overlay, and calls the proxy's own `ToggleAutoPickUp` (the game's flag is the one state, and its indicator follows it). With that flag on, the game itself picks only while E is held, so while it is on MKI Tools runs the proxy's own pickup (`DoPickup`, as a press of E) on the pickable you look at: an item pickup, or the proxy's foliage instance when it lies on your view ray within reach (the proxy keeps its last target after you look away).

## Options

MKI Tools has options under **Mods > MKI Tools** in the game's menus (SML mod configuration, `UMKIToolsConfiguration`, saved in the game's config folder):

- **Hand crafting multiplier** (whole numbers 1 to 10, default 1, key `HandCraftingSpeed`): how much crafting work each Craft Bench tick does, not how fast the ticks come. Each production step of the bench (`UFGWorkBench::Produce`) runs N times, and each can finish an item, so a tick finishes up to N items (the bench finishes at most one item per step; a single longer step did not help quick recipes). 1 leaves the game's own; a value typed outside 1-10 is held to the range.
- **Hand mining multiplier** (whole numbers 1 to 10, default 1, key `HandMiningSpeed`): how much ore each swing gives when mining a resource node by hand, not how fast the swings come. The swing animation paces the mining cycles (a shorter `mMiningCycleDuration` changed nothing live), so each completed cycle gives its reward (`AFGResourceMiner::Server_GiveMiningRewards`) N times.
- **Health regeneration speed** (whole numbers 1 to 10, default 1): how fast health regenerates on its own, as a multiple of the game's rate. The game's regeneration step (`AFGCharacterPlayer::TickHealthGeneration`) runs as if N times the time had passed; the wait after damage and the health curve (where regeneration stops) stay the game's own.
- **Movement speed bonus** (whole numbers 0 to 10, default 0; each step is 10%, so 10 is twice the game's speed; the maintainer found 2x still very fast and 10x ludicrous): how much faster the player moves than the game's own, multiplying the player movement component's top speed (`GetMaxSpeed`, every mode: walking, sprinting, crouching, swimming, jumping and falling) and its acceleration (`GetMaxAcceleration`, so the speed is reached as quickly and air control scales with it). Players only; creatures keep the game's own. Sliding (crouching at speed) and the game's own movement modes (ladders, hypertubes, ziplines, parachute) keep the game's speed, and a slide entered at a multiplied speed is held to the game's top speed for it (live: it threw the player half across the map). In multiplayer every player needs the same setting, or the host corrects their movement.
- **Collect nearby items** (on/off, default off) and **Collect radius** (whole numbers 1 to 10, default 1 = 5 m, the chainsaw's own area; 10 = 50 m): automatically picks up, around the player, berries, nuts and bacon agaric (foliage whose pickup gives `Desc_Berry`, `Desc_Nut` or `Desc_Shroom`; not leaves, wood, flowers or mycelia), items lying on the ground (`AFGItemPickup`: materials around crash sites, power slugs, somersloops, mercer spheres, creature remains), and the contents of dismantle and death crates (`AFGCrate`, `CT_DismantleCrate` and `CT_DeathCrate`, as far as the inventory has room). Foliage and ground items go through the game's own foliage pickup proxy (`SetPickupData` / `SetItemToPickup`, then `DoPickup`), one every 0.1 s, as a press of E does; crates are emptied on the host. `UMKICollectNearbySubsystem`.
- **Short conveyor lifts** (on/off, default on): the game builds a conveyor lift down to a lower height only when it snaps between two connections stacked above each other (`mMinimumHeightWithVerticalConnection`); otherwise its shortest is `mMinimumHeight`. With this on, the shortest everywhere is the first whole step (`mStepHeight`, 1 m) at or above that lower height (1.5 m): 2 m, the vanilla short lift (live: 1.5 m put every height off the 1 m grid). The lift still extends in the game's own steps up to its own maximum. Set on the lift hologram before each update of its top (`AFGConveyorLiftHologram::UpdateTopTransform`).
- **Inventory slots** (a button, not a value): shows the current inventory size and how many slots MKI Tools has added, and a **+10 slots** button adds 10 per press, up to 500 added per save (on the host; the button needs a loaded save). Can only grow: `AMKIPlayerSubsystem` keeps the slots added in each save (saved), adds them to `GetTotalPlayerInventorySlots`, and grows every player inventory below that count (at once and every second). Nothing lowers the count, and a player's own inventory is never resized smaller (a hook on `UFGInventoryComponent::Resize`), so items in the extra slots are never lost, even before the save's slots are known. SML's menu has no button, so `UMKIInventorySlotsOption` draws its own widget.
- **Chainsaw clearance radius** (whole numbers 1 to 10, default 1): how far around a sawn tree or bush the chainsaw also clears, as a multiple of the game's own radius (5 m, `mCollateralPickupRadius`): 1 is the game's own, 10 is 50 m. Applied each time the chainsaw is equipped. After the game's own clearing (`AFGChainsaw::RemoveCollateralFoliage`, which picks up only pickable plants and skips anything the chainsaw fells unless the chainsaw's area mode is on), MKI Tools clears the rest of the pickable and building-clearable foliage in the radius (such as short grass, without items) and, with "Chainsaw cuts boulders" on, the boulders, for their limestone together.
- **Chainsaw cuts boulders** (on/off, default on): the chainsaw cuts the boulders and rock clusters the game places as foliage (meshes under `/Game/FactoryGame/World/Environment/Rock/`), which the game otherwise leaves alone. MKI Tools answers yes to the game's own "is this foliage removable" check (`AFGFoliageRemovalSubsystem::IsRemovable`) for rock foliage only; bushes and trees keep the game's answer. The removal is the game's own: saved, synced in multiplayer, and permanent. With the option on, other things that clear removable foliage (such as building over it) clear boulders too.
- **Vegetation regrowth** (on/off, default off) and **Regrowth time (hours of play)** (whole numbers 1 to 10, default 3): cut trees, bushes and plants grow back at random, more likely the longer they have been gone. The game keeps every cut plant as a removed location in its foliage removal record (saved with the game). Once a minute the host gives each removed location a chance to grow back: a Weibull curve (shape 2) whose mean is the regrowth time, so few come back early and most around the average. The game keeps every foliage cell with cut plants loaded (live: 48 of 48), so a plant grows back into its cell straight away, but only more than 200 m from every player and, within 1 km, not in front of one. The record keeps only where the plant stood, so its yaw and size are drawn again from its foliage type; when the save next loads, the original plant is there exactly. In a cell that is not loaded, the plant just leaves the record. Boulders never regrow, nor does a spot within 3 m of a building. `AMKIRegrowthSubsystem` keeps its play-time clock and when it first saw each removed location in the save, and logs a summary every 10 minutes. In multiplayer, a client sees a regrown plant after reconnecting.

## Limestone

Removing rock gives limestone (`Desc_Stone`), on the server, once per removal, handed over through the game's own item pickup (dropped at the player and picked up, with the game's "+N" popup); what does not fit stays on the ground as an item drop:

- a boulder (Terrain mode or the chainsaw): 5 to 50, by its size;
- a level rock or arch (Terrain mode): 50 to 500, by its size. Restoring a rock through the bridge does not take the limestone back, and a removal made through the bridge gives none.

Boulders are the chainsaw's while "Chainsaw cuts boulders" is on (the maintainer's choice: the chainsaw shows the game's own pickup popup). With that option off, Terrain mode takes them instead: aimed at one, it is outlined, "Boulder: removal is permanent" shows under the crosshair, and holding fire removes it through the game's own foliage removal (`RemoveFoliageInstance`), so it cannot be restored.
